# Rock-Paper-Scissors in Nature: Population Dynamics of Cyclic Competition in Uta Stansburiana

n 1996, Sinervo & Lively documented a striking natural phe- nomenon: the three male throat-color morphs of the side- blotched lizard (Uta stansburiana) cycle through dominance in sequence over a five-year period, with no single morph ever permanently prevailing; a biological manifestation of the rock-paper-scissors game [1]. This paper applies the symmet- ric three-species Gause–Lotka–Volterra competition model of May & Leonard [2] to characterize the full landscape of popu- lation dynamics possible under intransitive cyclic dominance, and to assess which dynamical regimes are most consistent with biological observations of Uta stansburiana. Through a complete equilibrium and stability analysis, we identify three qualitatively distinct parameter regions: stable coexistence of all three morphs, competitive exclusion by a single morph, and sustained cycling with no stable equilibrium. We show that the cycling regime itself admits two qualitatively di!erent behaviors depending on the strength of competitive asymme- try: periodic limit cycles on a critical boundary, and growing- period cycling in the general case.
