# Evan Flaks

I first recall being excited by math in 5th grade when our school participated in the 24 Challenge – an arithmetic card game where players use four integers and basic operations (+, −, ÷, ×) exactly once to reach a total of 24, with the goal of being the first to solve the card. Although I was competitive, it wasn’t the competition that drew me in. It was the first time math felt like something I actually wanted to engage with, rather than something assigned to me. Looking back, I think the 24 Challenge gave me my first real taste of mathematical problem-solving as opposed to mathematical procedure. There’s a difference between executing an algorithm you’ve been taught and consciously searching for a solution. The satisfaction of uncovering an elusive solution is something I’ve pursued ever since through my coursework and ultimately my senior paper on nonlinear dynamical systems.

To those who claim “I am just not a math person,” I’d argue that’s impossible. Math is all around us and we interact with it everyday, whether we know it or not. When you split a bill, map out your day, adjust a recipe, or question the quality of a sale, you are doing math. When you are tapping your foot listening to music, you are doing math. Even the way your body calibrates balance and movement relies on ratios, angles, and predictions. You don’t opt into math; it’s embedded into how humans function. The question is not whether you are a math person, but whether you’re looking to see the math within and around you.
